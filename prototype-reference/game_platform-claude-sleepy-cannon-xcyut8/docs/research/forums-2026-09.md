## 1. What makes small web and mobile games succeed or fail
- **The first 10 seconds decide.** Web players "behave more like TikTok viewers than Steam users"; a game loses them if "the first ten seconds feel flat or the mobile layout asks players to rotate their phone" ([Poki blog, 2026-06-05](https://poki.com/blog/building-web-browser-games-2026)).
- **Instant fun, small builds:** under ≈ 20 MB, into fun in under 10 s, portrait and landscape; long intros, heavy tutorials, complex menus and slow unlocks hurt conversion ([Defold, 2026-06-02](https://defold.com/2026/06/02/Best-practices-when-building-for-the-web/)).
- **Portal gates are metrics:** Poki's Web Fit Test (≈ 10k players, 3–5 days; average game ≈ 70% conversion, 6+ min) ([docs](https://sdk.poki.com/web-fit-test.html)); CrazyGames Basic Launch (≥ 7 days and 500 plays; conversion = ≥ 1 min played; top games 80%+ conversion, 10+ min, D1 10–15%) ([docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)).
- **Small technical changes swing the algorithm.** Liquid Swarm (solo incremental roguelite, ≈ €31/day on CrazyGames after 3 flops): moving to WebGL2 raised bounce ≈ 5% (low-end devices) and cut revenue by ≈ a third; halving one upgrade's price nearly doubled playtime and raised retention 18% ([itch.io devlog, ~2026-07](https://mickaelbneron.itch.io/liquid-swarm/devlog/1579269/six-weeks-on-crazygames-my-incremental-roguelite-makes-31day-full-breakdown-of-whats-working-while-my-previous-three-games-flopped)).
- **The thumbnail and first screen are part of the game.** Larss: 0.4% CTR vs a 2–4% target; at 200×112 the thumbnail read as "just a grid" — "give people something to decode in half a second"; "Play now" into a tutorial "was killing conversion"; moving from Strategy to Puzzle fit its players better ([itch.io devlog, 2026-07](https://fxf8.itch.io/larss-io/devlog/1586849/launching-a-1v1-strategy-game-on-crazygames-04-ctr-and-what-im-learning)).
- **Trends and volume:** Playrea (25+ invisible mobile games, then web): "Brainrot Puzzle" 1M plays in a day; 5 games, 67M plays in 2025 ([Medium, early 2026](https://medium.com/@playrea/from-hobby-to-67-million-gameplays-on-poki-in-2025-df2c147cfb27)).
- **Launch barebones, monetize after:** OpenMyGame's Sea of Words reached Yandex's top 5 before ads and IAP were added ([Yandex Games, Medium](https://medium.com/yandexgames/how-a-game-studio-kickstarted-its-html5-journey-with-yandex-games-e8ac6350c4b6)).
- **How web players play:** most sessions 11–20 min (29%); 49% try 2–3 games per session; 90% do something else at the same time ([Poki State of Web Gaming 2026](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)).
- **Crowded:** ≈ 15,000 HTML5 games in H1 2025, ≈ 3× a year earlier, many AI-made ([PocketGamer.biz on Playgama data](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/more-than-15000-new-web-games-were-released-in-q2-2025/)).

## 2. Monetization that works, and what players hate
- **Midgame:** 40–60% of casual-game earnings on CrazyGames; the SDK paces ≈ 1 per 3 min, so request at every natural break and build no timers; **first ad after level 3–4, not level 1, to protect D1**; request on "Stage Clear", not while the next level loads ([CG midgame pacing](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/midgame-ads-pacing/)).
- **Rewarded:** highest eCPM, best accepted; rewards players actually want (revive, rare item) ([CG ad guide](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/ad-monetization-guide/)). Puzzle games lead CrazyGames' top-100 by earnings: 21 min, D1 6.2%, 8.9 ad impressions per play (4.2 rewarded); the best placement is a rewarded **hint** with a camera icon and a 1-min cooldown ([CG puzzle docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/monetizing-puzzle/)).
- **Poki rules:** `commercialBreak()` before each `gameplayStart()`, tell the player before a rewarded ad, mute audio, no internal ad timers, playable with an ad blocker ([requirements](https://sdk.poki.com/new-requirements.html)).
- **IAP on the web:** 2,000+ Yandex games have IAP; ads are still the main revenue ([App2Top, 2025-04](https://app2top.com/conferences/cii-minsk-2025-yandex-games-report-on-the-web-and-iap-monetization-280772.html)); CrazyGames IAP through Xsolla, invite-only ([docs](https://docs.crazygames.com/sdk/in-game-purchases/)).
- **What players hate:** players prefer rewarded to interstitials ≈ 4:1; they accept skippable (46.8%) and rewarded (41.4%) ads ([eMarketer / Attest, 2025-01](https://www.emarketer.com/content/faq-on-video-game-advertising--market-size--ad-formats--how-find-players-2026)); deceptive "X" buttons and too-frequent interstitials make players delete games ([AppHarbr via Playio](https://blog.playio.co/impact-of-ads-on-mobile-gamers) **[U]**).

## 3. Design patterns
- **Teach by playing**, not text ([Poki blog, 2026](https://poki.com/blog/building-web-browser-games-2026)).
- **Polish is a feature:** visual cohesion, consistent responsive UX, feedback loops, originality ([Poki](https://poki.com/blog/what-makes-high-quality-browser-game)).
- **Tune the economy before adding features** (Liquid Swarm above).
- **Daily hooks work when small and shareable:** Clues by Sam (browser daily logic puzzle, 2025-05) had 50,000+ daily players by early 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clues_By_Sam)).
- **Login bonuses disguise real engagement** — reward what players already enjoy ([dev.to](https://dev.to/sam_novak_574b07811e18495/when-monetization-kills-retention-real-post-mortems-on-failed-game-economy-design-17m5) **[U]**).
- **Retention is the weak point:** a solo browser TD saw 2% next-day return ([itch.io postmortem](https://itch.io/t/6645181/launch-week-postmortem-solo-browser-td-product-hunt-a-portal-rejection-and-a-flagged-show-hn-numbers-inside)).

## 4. Attitudes to AI-generated games
- **Players:** 85% of gamers below neutral on generative AI in games ([Quantic Foundry, 2025-12-18](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)); most "don't currently care", a vocal minority is against, refusers 22% → 25%+ ([GameSpot, ~2026-01](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/genai-in-games-most-players-just-dont-care-study-finds/1100-6538972/)); "AI slop" protests ([Washington Post, 2026-01-26](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/01/26/gamer-protests-ai-slop-backlash/)).
- **Steam:** AI-disclosed games were 33% of Q2 2026 releases but ≈ 10% of sales ([census of 53,597 games, ~2026-07](https://fragwyz.substack.com/p/three-years-of-ai-on-steam)). Disclosure covers content players consume; coding assistants are exempt ([PC Gamer, 2026-01](https://www.pcgamer.com/software/ai/steam-updates-ai-disclosure-form-to-specify-that-its-focused-on-ai-generated-content-that-is-consumed-by-players-not-efficiency-tools-used-behind-the-scenes/)).
- **Portals:** Poki — "AI should empower, not shortcut"; template-heavy AI games "may not meet" its standards ([docs](https://sdk.poki.com/working-with-ai.html)); CrazyGames screens clones and reskins; Google Play bans Repetitive Content ([policy](https://play.google.com/about/spam-min-functionality/spam/repetitive-content/)).

## 5. Kids games
- **Families rejections:** uncertified ad SDKs; the Advertising ID or AD_ID permission, often via a third-party SDK ([RevenueCat community](https://community.revenuecat.com/sdks-51/data-practices-of-google-families-policy-on-kids-app-2402)); ads not closable after 5 s; "inappropriate ad content" despite G rating ([AdMob group](https://groups.google.com/g/google-admob-ads-sdk/c/MQZ0I-PpXKQ)); anonymous chat banned for kids since 2026-07-15 ([Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17134731)).
- **Parents complain** about ads (≈ 35% of 1-star kids reviews **[U]**), subscription traps, ads that look like gameplay; a QUT study found deceptive patterns in 15 of 20 apps for ages 5–8 ([TechXplore, 2026-03](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-03-popular-kids-apps-deceptive-tactics.html)); FTC warnings on "ad-free" claims ([FTC, 2025-04](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2025/04/what-know-about-kids-video-games-ads-unexpected-payments)).
- **Parents praise** no ads, offline, calm, well narrated; Pok Pok: "no levels, no winning, and no losing" ([Boing Boing, 2025-04-01](https://boingboing.net/2025/04/01/finally-a-kids-app-that-wont-blast-your-eardrums-or-melt-your-brain.html) **[U]**).

## 6. First-time portal mistakes
- Sending a prototype ("needs content and polishing… looks like a prototype") ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/my-game-got-rejected-just-2-hours-dmitrii-rigin)); a game "too close to existing titles" ([itch.io](https://itch.io/t/6645181/launch-week-postmortem-solo-browser-td-product-hunt-a-portal-rejection-and-a-flagged-show-hn-numbers-inside)).
- Tutorial-first entry, the wrong category, an unreadable thumbnail (Larss).
- Heavy tech on low-end devices (WebGL2, big WASM, long loads).
- Breaking SDK rules: own ad timers, ads before gameplay, audio during ads, an ad-blocker wall.

## The 10 most actionable rules
1. Gameplay within 10 s of the click; teach in the first round; 70–80%+ still playing after a minute.
2. Small and plain: well under 20 MB, Canvas/WebGL1-safe, tested on low-end Android browsers.
3. Portrait and landscape; never ask to rotate.
4. A thumbnail readable in half a second at 200×112; the category whose players know the mechanic.
5. A break every 1–3 min; ads there, paced by the SDK; none before round 3–4.
6. Rewarded ads opt-in and useful (hint, revive, ×2), with a camera icon and a cooldown; never a paywall, never in kids games.
7. Tune numbers with data before adding content.
8. Save progress and one honest reason to return tomorrow (a daily seed, an unlock track); no bare login bonuses.
9. Kids: no manipulation, no identifiers, certified SDKs only, parent gate before purchases and links.
10. Each game clearly its own and polished; never a reskin; disclose AI assets where asked.
